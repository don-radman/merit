# Merit — go-to-market

A plan, not a wish list. Who first, how we land, how it grows, what we charge, what could
stop us. Written so a slide can be lifted from each section.

## The one-line position

Merit is the distribution rail for tokenized funds: the introduction is recorded the moment
a client invests, the fee vests and is paid privately, and the rate stays between the adviser
and the fund. Merit Connect fits it into the access portal a manager already runs.

## Who we sell to, in order

1. **Fund managers and platforms with tokenized funds on Stellar.** They buy the system.
   Design partners first: two managers or platforms who already have, or are building, an
   investor portal and distribute through advisers. Candidates are the smaller issuers and
   platforms rather than the largest (a Franklin Templeton has its own distribution; a
   Conduit, an Etherfuse or a regional manager tokenizing a first fund does not).
2. **Distribution partners.** Family offices, multi-family offices, wealth managers,
   placement agents and independent advisers who already introduce clients to funds and
   currently chase the fee. They do not buy Merit; they ask for it. Every adviser who has a
   book with one fund asks the next fund why it does not have one.
3. **HNW ambassadors.** Existing investors who refer peers into a fund they are already in.
   The same link, the same record, a different programme: disclosed, capped, consented by the
   fund, and run as a programme rather than a product. This is the piece Co/Unity knows how to
   run and the piece that turns a fund's investor base into its distribution.

## The wedge

Land with one fund on one platform through Merit Connect, with Co/Unity running the first
introducer programme around it. Not a platform sale on day one. One fund, five advisers, the
first payout run on chain, a case study with real numbers the fund agrees to publish.

Then expand across the platform: the integration is done once, every fund on it can switch
Merit on. Then across platforms: the advisers carry their book to the next fund and ask for it.

## How it grows (the loop)

Adviser gets a book with fund A → brings the same clients to fund B → asks fund B for Merit →
fund B joins → fund B's other advisers get books → repeat. The adviser's single book across
funds is the growth engine; the second integration is worth more than the first.

## Phases

| Phase | When | Goal | Done when |

## What we charge (options to test with design partners)

- **Platform fee** per fund or per platform, monthly: the ledger, the book, the payout run,
  the auditor view.
- **A share of settled fees**: a small percentage of each placement fee Merit pays out. Aligns
  us with volume; priced so the adviser never notices and the fund sees it as cheaper than
  the back office it replaces.
- **Programme fees** through Co/Unity: design and operation of the introducer and ambassador
  programmes around Merit. The service is separate from the product by design (brandbook,
  "Service").

Start with the first two for design partners at a founding-customer rate; the programme fee
is Co/Unity's existing business.

## Channels

- **Dan's conversations.** The family offices and wealth managers already talking to Co/Unity
  are distribution partners on day one, and the fastest way to a design partner is an adviser
  who says "my fund should have this".
- **Stellar ecosystem.** Stellar Community Fund application; SDF's institutional and RWA team;
  Meridian; OpenZeppelin co-marketing on the confidential token (we are a live use of it).
- **Content.** "Credit where it's due": short, factual stories of an introduction that was
  paid on time. The public-view toggle is the demo that explains the product in one click.
- **Events.** Wealth-tech and private-markets conferences in Singapore, Dubai, London and
  Zurich, where the advisers are. Meridian for the Stellar side.

## Risks and how we hold them

- **Regulation of introducers and placement agents** differs by jurisdiction (finder rules,
  appointed-representative regimes, licensing of placement activity). Merit records and pays;
  the fund stays responsible for who it may pay and how it discloses it. We build with that
  boundary explicit and bring compliance counsel into the first design partnership.
- **Confidentiality claims.** Until Stellar's zero-knowledge verifier ships, we say "the
  amount is absent from the ledger", never "private", and we do not take real money through it.
- **Concentration.** One platform could be most of the book. The adviser's cross-fund book is
  the hedge: it pulls the next fund in.
- **Chain choice.** We are Stellar-first because the funds, the asset controls, the passkey
  verification and the confidential token are here. The design does not depend on anything
  only Stellar has, which is a strength to state, not hide.

## What success looks like in a year

Ten funds with Merit on, a hundred advisers with a book, a published number for capital
introduced through Merit links, and every payout on time. Measured by: funds live, advisers
with books, introduced subscriptions, fees settled, days from vesting to payout.
